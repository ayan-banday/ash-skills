# Rejected Shapes

Shapes that failed before, with the reason each failed. Check every pair against this list before showing it.

## 1. Coined concept name plus aphorism
**Headline:** The Convergence Problem
**Subhead:** You are not scattered. You are early.
**Why it failed:** A stranger cannot say what they would learn. The coined term needs its own explanation, and the subhead is a slogan with nothing under it. It also uses negative parallelism.
**Fix:** Name the actual claim or structure in plain words.

## 2. Subhead that restates the headline
**Headline:** 3 Reasons Your Focus Routine Keeps Failing
**Subhead:** 3 reasons your focus routine keeps failing and what to do.
**Why it failed:** The subhead adds zero information.
**Fix:** Use the subhead for the reader, the proof, the contents, or the objection.

## 3. First person with nothing behind it
**Headline:** I Figured Out the One Trick to Better Focus
**Why it failed:** "I" with no story, number, or cost. It is a claim wearing a costume.
**Fix:** Add a before and after number, or switch to a Mode B headline.

## 4. Ungrounded abstraction
**Headline:** Rethinking What Really Matters
**Subhead:** A new way to think about what really matters in your work.
**Why it failed:** It could sit on 10 unrelated pieces. It names no reader, no number, and no cost.
**Fix:** Anchor it to a specific thing the reader does or loses.

## 5. Dangling referent
**Headline:** This Changes Everything About How You Write
**Why it failed:** A stranger has to guess what "this" refers to.
**Fix:** Name the thing: "This 6-Part Structure Changes How You Write a Book."

## 6. Guarantee language
**Headline:** The Routine That Guarantees You'll Never Procrastinate Again
**Why it failed:** It promises certainty no piece can deliver and breaks trust on the first read.
**Fix:** State the cost or the stake: "3 Reasons Your Focus Routine Keeps Failing."

## 7. Negative parallelism
**Headline:** It's Not About Discipline, It's About Design
**Why it failed:** The "not X, but Y" frame is a recognizable writing tic.
**Fix:** State the point directly: "Discipline Is a Byproduct of Design."

## 8. Hedging opener
**Headline:** Here's the Thing About Focus Routines
**Why it failed:** It announces that a point is coming and delivers none.
**Fix:** Lead with the point: "Your Focus Routine Was Never the Problem."

## 9. Label in place of a situation
**Headline:** Why High Performers Stay Stuck
**Subhead:** What smart people get wrong about growth.
**Why it failed:** "High performers" and "smart people" name nobody. The pair fits every piece in the niche.
**Fix:** Name what the reader does: "Why Founders With 4 Clients and a Full Week Stay Stuck."

## 10. Padded number
**Headline:** 5 Reasons Writing a Book Takes So Long
**Why it failed:** When the material names only 2 reasons, the number is padding made to look like a listicle.
**Fix:** Count what is really there, or tag an invented number [invented].

## 11. Clickbait with nothing inside
**Headline:** The Shocking Truth About Time Management
**Subhead:** You will not believe what the research says.
**Why it failed:** No number, no reader, no claim. The subhead promises mystery and says nothing.
**Fix:** Put the surprising fact in the headline.

## 12. Same shape across the set
**Headlines:** "Why X Fails" / "Why Y Fails" / "Why Z Fails" / "Why W Fails"
**Why it failed:** A set of headlines that open the same way reads like a template. The variety gate fails.
**Fix:** Vary the opening word and sentence shape across the set.

## 13. Spelled-out numbers
**Headline:** Two Things You Need to Ghostwrite in Any Industry
**Why it failed:** Numbers in headlines are always numerals.
**Fix:** "The 2 Things You Need to Ghostwrite in Any Industry."

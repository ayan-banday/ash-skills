# Writing a Whole Week (or the Whole Course)

Open this when the job is more than one module. It's the process that produced the Agriculture Edition's round 2 (October 2026), which Ash judged good enough to move on from. A single writer drafting six days alone drifted: the running student's story contradicted itself across weeks, and examples broke the rules their own day taught. Fresh reviewers caught both. The steps below keep that from happening.

## 1. Plan the week before any day

Write `plan.md` in the week's folder:

- **The week's big question,** in words a newcomer already knows.
- **A and B:** where the student starts and ends this week, and **what skipping the week would cost them**.
- **For each day:** the problem the student has when the day starts (left by yesterday), the day's question, A → B, how the running student does it and what surprises them, what the student has at the end, the skills it builds and the learning mode.
- **Hand-offs:** what each day hands the next, and what the week hands the next week.

Done when every day's problem is named sharply and each day's end is the next day's start.

## 2. Keep one fact sheet for the running student's story

Before more than one week is written, make a fact sheet (the Agriculture Edition's is `gia-canon.md`): every person's name and role, every offer version as each week leaves it, every price and its source, the names of the tabs and documents in the student's doc, the labels the course uses, and the story beat of each week. **When two weeks disagree, decide once in the fact sheet and change the weeks.** Every writer and reviewer reads it.

## 3. Write each day

Follow `SKILL.md` for each module. For a page with drawings, Ash's Week 1 page is the format: write the Markdown first (lint it), then the HTML with the same classes, and screenshot it at laptop and phone width before you finish.

## 4. A fresh reviewer for every week

The writer never reviews their own week. Give a fresh agent: the course's rules page, Ash's feedback in his own words, the benchmark page, the outline, the fact sheet, and the week's files. **Withhold the writer's notes.** The reviewer renders the pages, then judges every day in this order:

1. Does each section fully answer its heading's question?
2. Does the day solve the problem yesterday left, and hand tomorrow one concrete thing?
3. Is it as plain as the benchmark? Quote every filler, brochure or AI-sounding sentence.
4. Does the running student's example show exactly how to do the day, obey the day's rule, match the fact sheet, and teach a surprise?
5. Every kind of customer in it?
6. Does every phrase make sense on its own to a newcomer?
7. Pictures after their words, one idea each, readable at phone width?
8. A "how it looks" picture for every multi-step Do?
9. Real quotes exact and sourced; real people named by place or role?
10. The rules: question headings, no week map or teach-back, Applied and Excelled, running day numbers.

The reviewer replies with ranked fixes, each with the day, the section and the concrete change, and edits nothing. The writer applies every fix.

## 5. A consistency pass across weeks

When all weeks are fixed, one more fresh agent reads the course in order, as a student would, against the fact sheet. It fixes small mismatches directly (names, quoted offer lines, prices, tab names, Tomorrow lines and Next links) and lists anything that needs a content decision for Ash.

## 6. Report to Ash in plain words

Lead with the link. Then what changed, and only the decisions he needs to make, each in a sentence he can answer by number. Use no internal terms (fact sheet IDs, agent names, "canon", "round 2 brief").

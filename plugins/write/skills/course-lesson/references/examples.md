## Day 6: Where Are Farmers and Agri Entrepreneurs Already Talking About My Problem?

Till now you have five tabs in your Offer Document:

- **Day 1:** a goal, and the blocker in its way
- **Day 2:** the part of agriculture where that blocker sits
- **Day 3:** your problem in one sentence, each part marked Known or Guess
- **Day 4:** one narrow group, and a fix for your worst wrong belief
- **Offer:** Offer v1, five lines on what you'll sell, to whom, and for how much

Every one of those five lines is still **your guess**, because nobody who has the problem has read it. This week answers one question: how do you find out **if people want your solution,** before you **spend money on it?**

Today you collect what farmers and agri entrepreneurs have **already said** about your problem, in their own words. It builds **evidence seeking**: finding out what's true from the people who live it.

## What happens if I build on a guess?

Say you spend a month and **₹15,000** building an app that tells tomato farmers when to spray their crop. Then you show it to farmers, and they say the seed shop owner already tells them, free, when they buy the spray.

The month and the money are gone, and you're back at Day 5. Checking first takes **40 minutes** and costs nothing.

> **Drawing:** A student holds a phone app, with a coin and a calendar above him that read "₹15,000, 1 month". An arrow leads to a farmer, who points past him to the seed shop, where the owner behind the counter says "spray on Friday".

## Do I need to meet a farmer before I can learn anything?

You may think you can't learn anything about farmers until you sit with one. So you:

- **Wait for a free weekend** to visit a village, and the weekend never comes
- **Ask ChatGPT "what do farmers want?"** and copy its tidy list into your notes
- **Ask friends in Bangalore** whether your offer sounds good, and hear "nice idea"

ChatGPT's list is a guess too. No farmer's village, number or words sit behind it, so **you've swapped your guess for its guess.**

Farmers already talk about their problems in public, every day. **They post videos, comment under other people's videos and talk to reporters.** You can collect it all tonight from your hostel bed, and you don't even have to read it first.

## Who are agri entrepreneurs, and why do I need them too?

**Agri entrepreneurs** run a business in agriculture: a startup that makes farm devices, a cold room, a seed and fertiliser shop, a milk collection centre. You need them for two reasons:

- **They already tried to fix your problem,** so they know what broke, what farmers asked for, and what farmers paid.
- **They might be your customer.** If your offer is for a cold room owner, the cold room owner's words are the ones you collect.

Farmers tell you **the pain.** Agri entrepreneurs tell you **what happened when someone tried to fix it.**

## What is Gia checking this week?

On Day 4 Gia's fix was to **hear from at least three more farmers who make the night walk.** This is the Offer v1 she's checking:

> **Who it's for:** Small borewell farmers near Kunigal
> **What they get:** Switch the pump on from home, with no walk to the field at night
> **What's included:** A small box fitted to the pump's switch, and an app to turn it on and off
> **How it's delivered:** Fitted in one visit, and the farmer downloads the app
> **What they pay:** Rs 4,000 for the box and Rs 100 a month, paid by the farmer

All five lines are her guess. This week she finds out which ones farmers agree with.

## Where do I find what they say?

Three places hold most of it:

- **Kannada and Hindi YouTube.** Farmers film their fields, test products on camera, and comment in their own language. Each video gives you two things to copy: **the transcript**, which is every word said in the video, typed out by YouTube, and **the comments** under it.
- **News stories that quote farmers.** Reporters travel to villages and print what farmers told them. Look on PARI, the People's Archive of Rural India, The Better India, Deccan Herald, The Hindu and Prajavani.
- **Interviews and posts by agri entrepreneurs.** Founders tell The Better India, YourStory and LinkedIn why they started and what went wrong. The comments under their posts hold more founders.

If your customer is an agri entrepreneur, the same three places work. A cold room owner shows up in news stories about stored vegetables going bad, and in YouTube tours of cold rooms.

## What do I type into the search bar?

The words you search with decide who you find. A farmer never types "smart irrigation solution", so that search only shows you company ads.

**Search with a full sentence about the farmer's day,** the way he'd ask it. Read it back: a stranger should understand it, and learn something from it.

**Good pick: "why farmers water their fields at night".** You land on videos and news stories from people who live it, and the sentence alone tells you that power comes at night.

**Bad pick: "IoT irrigation automation India".** You land on company websites selling IoT, meaning internet-connected devices, which tell you what sellers think.

> **Drawing, as two good and bad pick cards:** Left card: a search bar reading "why farmers water their fields at night", an arrow, and a farmer with a speech bubble. Right card: a search bar reading "IoT irrigation automation India", an arrow, and a shop window of product ads with price tags.

Then search the same sentence in Kannada and Hindi. Ask ChatGPT to translate it for you:

> ರೈತರು ರಾತ್ರಿ ಹೊಲಕ್ಕೆ ನೀರು ಹಾಯಿಸುವುದು ಏಕೆ?
> किसान रात में खेतों में पानी क्यों देते हैं?

For agri entrepreneurs, write the sentence from their side: **"startup that lets farmers switch on their pump from a phone".** If your customer is a cold room owner, try "why cold room owners lose vegetables in summer".

## How do I collect it without reading it all?

**Select everything, copy it, and paste it raw.** Don't read it, sort it, fix the spelling or tidy it. The GPT does the sorting.

Add a tab called **"Day 6: What Farmers Say"** to your Offer Document. Do this on a laptop, because copying from the YouTube app takes far longer.

For each YouTube video:

1. **Open the video,** click "...more" under its title, then click **"Show transcript"**.
2. **Drag from the first line of the transcript to the last,** press Ctrl+C, and paste it into your tab with Ctrl+Shift+V, which pastes it as plain text. Put the video's link on the line above it.
3. **Scroll down to the comments,** and keep scrolling for a minute so more of them load.
4. **Drag from the first comment to the last one loaded,** copy, and paste it under the transcript.

> **Drawing, how it looks on YouTube:** A YouTube page. On the right, the transcript panel with every line highlighted blue as selected. Below the video, the comments, highlighted blue from the first to the last. Numbers 1 to 4 mark the steps.

**No "Show transcript" button?** Many Kannada videos don't have one. Copy the comments only.

For a news story or a founder's interview, press **Ctrl+A** to select the whole page, copy it, and paste it under its link. Menus and ads come along too, and that's fine.

Aim for **three videos, three news stories and one or two founders.** Your tab will look like a mess, which means you did it right.

> **Drawing, how the tab looks:** A Google Doc with tabs down the side, "Day 6: What Farmers Say" highlighted. The page holds raw pasted text: a link, "Comments 214 Sort by", grey lines of comments with "Reply" between them, then a pasted news story where the word "Advertisment" and a photo caption run straight into a farmer's words, with his surname greyed out.

## What do I ask the GPT?

Open the course GPT, type "Day 6", paste your whole tab, and ask one question **with your problem in it:**

> What do farmers say about **[your problem]**? And what do agri entrepreneurs say about it?

The problem in the question tells the GPT what to look for. Without it, the GPT sums up everything you pasted, including the ads and the menus.

Gia asked, "What do farmers say about **walking to the field at night to switch on the pump?** And what do agri entrepreneurs say about it?" It replied:

> **What farmers say:**
> - Power comes at night, so they water at night. One farmer nearly stepped on a snake in the dark.
> - Their motors go bad about twice a year, and one farmer spent ₹10,000 to ₹12,000 a year on repairs.
> - One farmer travelled 4 km several times a day just to water the field.
> - Someone asking a seller about a phone pump switch wanted a number to call when it breaks.
>
> **What agri entrepreneurs say:**
> - A founder built a phone switch because his grandfather walked a kilometre to the pump at midnight.
> - Another founder had to redesign his switch because farm power is so unsteady.

> **Drawing, how it looks in the GPT:** A chat window. Gia's message shows "Day 6", a pasted block marked "Day 6: What Farmers Say, 31 pages", and her question. The GPT's reply shows the two short lists.

If the GPT says your paste is too long, download the tab with File → Download → .docx, and attach the file instead.

Gia now knows **what farmers and founders say about her problem,** in 40 minutes, without leaving her room. She pasted the reply at the top of her Day 6 tab.

## Collect what farmers and agri entrepreneurs say (40 minutes)

1. Add a tab called **"Day 6: What Farmers Say"** to your Offer Document.
2. Write your search sentence in a farmer's words, then in Kannada or Hindi, and one from an agri entrepreneur's side.
3. Copy everything raw from **three videos, three news stories and one or two agri entrepreneurs.**
4. Open the course GPT, type "Day 6", paste your tab, and ask your question with your problem in it. Paste its answer at the top of your tab.
5. The answer ends with an **Evidence** part. Keep it as it is.

**Applied:** your tab holds raw text from farmers and at least one agri entrepreneur, and the GPT's answer is about your problem.

**Excelled:** **you also read your problem sentence to one person who lives it,** and pasted what they said, word for word, under Evidence.

**Short on time?** Copy one video and one news story, ask the same question, and add the rest on the weekend.

## What Day 6 shows

- **Every heading is the student's next question** and its section answers it. The recap names the problem and what ignoring it costs (a month and ₹15,000). Farmers and agri entrepreneurs appear in every section. Gia's example shows each click, the exact GPT question with her problem in it, and the answer. Stuck points get one line each where they happen (no transcript button, a paste that's too long). A go-do-it day runs long because it shows each step.
- **Mistakes sit inside examples and rules.** No module has a "mistakes" section.
- **Objections sit where they arise,** in a sentence under the example they belong to.
- **The Do is always explain, link, save.** The GPT holds the case, the interview, and the summary.

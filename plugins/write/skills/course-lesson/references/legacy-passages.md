# Older Teaching Passages

Contents: two short sample passages written before the daily-module rules existed, and how to use them.

These passages are good voice samples: spoken, concrete, and easy to skim. They predate the module rules, so where they conflict with `SKILL.md`, `SKILL.md` wins. They use "(Made up...)" labels and some rhetorical questions that the module rules now drop. Use them to hear the voice, never as structure.

## Passage 1: finding a problem

Most people think a business starts with a **big idea**. You wait for one to show up, and while you wait, nothing happens.

Businesses usually start smaller than that. Someone notices a **problem** that keeps annoying a group of people, and they try to fix it for them.

So where do you find one? Look at your own week:

- **Something that wasted your time**, like a queue, a form, or a group chat that never decides anything
- **Something that cost you money** you didn't plan to spend
- **Something you had to ask three people about** before you got an answer

Each of those is a problem, and each one probably belongs to more people than just you.

Say you spent an hour hunting for a clean, affordable PG near campus. Plenty of new students do the same every year. The **problem** is the hunt, it belongs to **new students**, and a good **result** is finding a place in one afternoon.

Now try yours:

1. Pick one annoyance from your last three days.
2. Write who else has it.
3. Write what "fixed" would look like for them.

> **Problem:** \_\_\_
> **Who has it:** \_\_\_
> **Fixed looks like:** \_\_\_

You haven't got an idea yet, and you don't need one. You've got a problem and a person, and that's where ideas come from.

## Passage 2: the handoffs behind a food

When you eat a sandwich, how many people helped it get to you? Usually a lot more than you'd guess.

Someone **grew** the tomato. Someone **collected** it from many farms, someone **moved** it, someone **sold** it, and someone in the canteen **put it between two slices of bread**. Each of them has a job, and each job can get hard.

That's why agriculture is a good place to look for problems. It's a long chain of handoffs, and wherever one goes wrong, somebody loses time or money.

Try it with something you ate today:

1. Write the food at the top.
2. List the hands it passed through, from farm to you. Guess where you're not sure.
3. Circle the handoff you think is hardest, and write why.

> **Food:** tomato sandwich
> **Hands:** farmer → collector → truck → market seller → canteen → me
> **Hardest handoff:** the truck, because tomatoes might soften on the way
> **Who it hurts:** the market seller, who could lose part of the load

A guess you can check is how you start, and checking it is where it gets interesting.

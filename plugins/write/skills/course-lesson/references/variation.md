# Variation

Ash's note: the structure and voice stay the same, and the wording should stay fresh the way Dan Koe's does. If every module opens "Most people believe...", the course reads like a template, and students feel it by Day 3.

Contents: what stays fixed, what can move, the opener catalogue, the connector and closer catalogue, a rotation plan, and moves we skip.

## What stays fixed

- The skeleton order of jobs (see `structure.md`)
- The plain, spoken, 7th-grade voice
- The sentence and formatting rules in `SKILL.md`
- The bold-lead bullets, the labeled examples, the GPT-based Do

## What can move

- **How the opener starts** (the catalogue below)
- **Which example leads,** as long as the simplest one comes first
- **Where an objection sits,** as long as it follows the claim that raises it
- **How the bridge is phrased**
- **How tomorrow is teased**
- **A single de-pressure line** when the day is abstract or heavy

## Opener catalogue

Pick one per day and do not repeat it within the week. Each shows the Dan Koe move it comes from and how we write it.

**1. The common belief (use sparingly)**
Koe uses "Most people..." often. We use it at most once a week, on the day that breaks the biggest belief.
> Most people believe a business starts with an idea.

**2. The student's own voice**
Koe addresses the reader's private doubt directly. We state what the student may be thinking.
> If you study computer science, design, or law, you may think agriculture has no room for you.

**3. A short scene**
Koe often shows the wrong move in action before naming it ("Illustrate what most people do wrong"). We drop the student into a two-sentence scene with an impact line.
> Say you notice the canteen queue is long every day, so you spend three weeks building a pre-order app. Then you ask the people in the queue, and most of them say they don't mind.

**4. A direct instruction**
Koe: "Stop trying to create a fancy product that nobody has ever invented."
> Stop looking for a big idea. Start looking at what is already annoying someone near you.

**5. The recap and turn**
Koe: "So far, we have a product idea, customer avatar, and offer strategy."
> So far you have a link in the chain and a person who lives there. Today you find out if their problem deserves your time.

**6. The cost first**
Koe: "If you don't nail the introduction, people stop reading."
> If you pick the wrong problem, you lose a month on something nobody pays to fix.

**7. The question the student already has**
Koe answers questions readers send in ("Should I start a newsletter if I'm just starting out?").
> "Do I have to know farming to start a business in it?" Short answer: no, and today shows why.

**8. The surprising claim**
Koe: "Many people will not like this."
> Your first offer should be wrong. That is the plan.

**9. The reframe as a better question**
Koe: "The question isn't 'What do I sell?' The question is 'How do I create an offer they can't refuse?'" We use the idea without the negative parallelism.
> The better question is "Who is stuck, and on what?", because the idea comes after the answer.

**10. The permission opener**
Koe gives permission to take long or drop the task if overwhelmed. Use it when the day is hard.
> Today's idea may feel fuzzy. That is fine, and it will sharpen by Friday.

## Connector and closer catalogue

**Between sections**
- A plain next step, with no connector at all (the default)
- A question, only when a real reader would ask it
- A one-line recap before a new idea: "You have the goal and the blocker. Now you need to know which blocker to fix."

**Closing the module (tomorrow line)**
- Name the next question: "Tomorrow you check your own thinking. What do first-time founders get wrong?"
- Name the tension: "You might think agriculture means farming. Tomorrow shows how much wider it is."
- Name the payoff: "Tomorrow you find the link that fits you."

## Rotation plan for a week

Vary the opener across the week so no two days feel alike.

| Day | Opener type | Example in this course |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Common belief | "Most people believe a business starts with an idea." |
| 2 | Student's own voice | "If you study computer science... you may think agriculture has no room for you." |
| 3 | Scene | The canteen queue and the app |
| 4 | Cost first or direct instruction | "If you ask only friends, you hear what you want to hear." |
| 5 | Recap and turn | "So far you have a problem, a link, and a list of mistakes. Today you put them in one offer." |

## Moves we skip

- **"Here's the critical mindset shift:"** Koe uses it. Ash rejected it because it is a lead-in.
- **Negative parallelism** such as "The question isn't X, it's Y". Rewrite it as a positive statement.
- **One-word questions** such as "Why?" followed by an answer. Write the full question.
- **Hype punctuation** such as "Exciting." or "We are now moving onto monetization!" Keep the warmth in the plain wording.
- **Long essay lessons.** Koe's median is about 1,300 words, with some at 3,000. Our modules are shorter because the reader has less time.

## The one test

**Keep a sentence only if a 7th grader gets it on the first read, and it adds a fact, an example or an action.** [3+] (course-lesson "Why the rules are strict"; brief-page writing-rules "The one test")

## Rules for every case

**Clear**
- **Plain, spoken words,** like a friendly senior talking in a corridor. Contractions. Plain verbs: find, spot, check, pick, pay. [3+] (course-lesson "Voice"; pitch-site copy-rules: "Would Ash say it this way, out loud?")
- **Meaning first, the name after.** Say what happens, then "This is called X". Explain every term the first time, even "database". [3+] (course-lesson; break-into-field writing-rules "A to B, never B to A")
- **Specific beats general.** Name the tool, the place, the number, the day. "Search YouTube and Instagram for startup ideas", never "research the idea". [3+]
- **Exact numbers,** the same everywhere. Count what you list. "20 minutes", never "a while". [3+]
- **Name things plainly,** the way the reader would say them: "Follow-ups", never "Loose people". (Ash, Oct 25)
- **One name per thing.** A need, a term or a person keeps the same short name all the way through. [3+] (pitch-site writing-rules; domain-modeling)

**Short**
- **Paragraphs of 1 to 3 sentences.** On study pages, 60 words at most, with a bold lead-in. [3+]
- **Bullets for anything with parts.** Nested, plain words. Shown to Ash, **a list holds 3 items at most**; past 3, group under up to 3 bold parents. In files, 5 at most. (Ash, Nov 2: "A list never goes past three items"; Fuji rulebook 2 "Lists everywhere")
- **Say it once.** No counting setups ("one of two things"), no lines that comment on the page ("All three are real"), no closing recap. [3+]
- **Bold one or two key phrases per paragraph or bullet, never a whole sentence** (a bold sentence is the commonest slip). Reading only the bold gives the point. [3+]

**Stands alone**
- **Every heading, sentence and question makes sense read alone.** No bare "this", "it", "the above". Repeat the fact instead of pointing back. [3+] (Fuji rulebook 3: "Concise is fine; vague is not")
- **Headings are the question the reader would ask,** naming the thing and the situation. Never a slot ("Overview", "Next steps"), a slogan, a clever label ("It's the fountain"), an announcement ("We heard you"), a template label ("Gap:", "Sharper:") or a count. "I fucking hate announcements. They're never helpful." **Questions are the default everywhere** (pages, pitch sites, slides, prototypes, docs, printed documents): the heading names the problem the section solves, as a question. A takeaway headline ("New hires take 9 days to set up") is the second option, only when Ash asks. (Ash, Nov 11) [3+] (course-lesson; brief-page; polished-doc "Headings"; Ash, Oct 27 and Nov 2: his most repeated correction)
- **Each section answers its heading in full.** The reader can say the answer in 2 sentences and act today. [3+]
- **Put the impact in the same sentence as the claim.** "A company can't open two new cities if a fifth of its fruit rots." [3+]

**Only what was asked**
- **Add nothing nobody asked for:** no extra tips, "Add your first idea" boxes, analogies or marketing lines. "Remove the hedges... don't have anything that sounds like marketing material. I'm talking to my team." (Ash, Oct 27)
- **A metaphor only for an idea too hard to say plainly.** Otherwise a plain line, then a mini example. ("No analogies, no water, nothing", Ash, Oct 27; study-bba writing-rules "A metaphor only where the idea is hard")
- **Leave out your tools and internal names:** "make them fill a form", never the form tool's name. (Ash, Nov 2; pitch-site copy-rules)
- **Examples use one running character with a plain made-up name,** never a real person's name. (Ash, Oct 28 and 29)

**Honest**
- **Never invent a statistic, quote, credential or real company's result.** Made-up examples, names and small numbers are fine, and you list them for Ash. [3+]
- **Facts come from Ash, the spec or a written source.** Check voice-typed names against something written. [3+]
- **Say exactly what's uncertain** instead of hedging ("about 40 minutes" is fine; "might" is not). [3+]

## For Ash

**What Ash reads in chat**
- **Understand before you build.** Say back what you understood in plain words, and never ask what he already told you. "You understand before you go create things." "Why do you need to ask me that?" (Ash, Nov 1)
- **Say what it means for him,** never only what it is. "Could you just tell me what that means for you?" (Ash, Nov 1)
- **No word he'd have to remember from earlier.** If a line leans on context ("the name-it thing, the three cards"), repeat the fact. "I have no idea what the fuck you're talking about." (Ash, Nov 2)
- **A reply that carries a page:** the link, one line on what it shows, the guesses. Nothing the page already says.
- **The answer or the thing first.** Never "Now I'll...", a checklist of what you did, or a summary of the chat back to him. (course-lesson "How to deliver"; teach-side-chat rule 12)
- **Lead with your best guess.** "Ash reacts faster to a guess than to a blank." (pitch-site intake)
- **Questions:** numbered, each standing alone with its own context, a few lettered options, the recommended one first so "A" accepts it. No question-picker widget. (grilling; Fuji rulebook 3)
- **Say back what you understood** in short caveman bullets before asking. (Fuji rulebook 3)
- **Name an option, then say it plainly:** "a deck you deal: cards sit in a stack and you flick the top one away". A name alone lost him. (pitch-site intake)
- **Facts are your job, decisions are his.** Never ask him something you could look up. (grilling)
- **Skill or command suggestions:** 3 lines at most, the command in bold. (which-skill)

**A study result or report** (he tested a hypothesis or started a run from his phone)
- **The verdict first, in one line:** holds, doesn't hold, or not sure yet.
- **Up to three reasons,** one line each, each a new fact with what it means for him. Never restate the verdict line; two real reasons beat three padded ones.
- **One next step** he can take, with what's done when.
- Sources and method behind a tap. Shape and look: "Phone study page" in `/visualize:match-ash-design` (`../../../visualize/skills/match-ash-design/references/design.md`). (Ash, Nov 2, via the grilling-layers session)

**A study page or book** (he's learning a topic)
- Open with "Where you are now" (2 or 3 bullets, each with its cost), then "After this, you can", written as what changes in his work. (break-into-field book-shape)
- **Each idea:** a plain sentence he could act on, then a far-away example (a field nothing like his), then in his world (Udyaan, Fuji, Shasta, the micro-SaaS). (teach-side-chat; design-like-expert)
- **Numbers he can picture** ("about an hour of video, not 20"). A metaphor only where the idea is hard. (teach-side-chat; study-bba)
- People's names, dates and quotes only under Sources. (break-into-field writing-rules)

**A prototype or a plan of action**
- **Show the outcome first,** then the steps. Each step says what's true when it's done. (Fuji rulebook 1 "Done when")
- **Up to 5 steps;** more becomes phases. (Fuji rulebook 2)
- **Fits one phone screen or two:** each step is its outcome plus at most 2 bullets and its done-when. Every step names who does it.
- After it, 3 lines at most: what you guessed, what you invented.
- **Cheapest first.** Before a big build, show a summary of what you'll recommend, or a quick page from a cheap model (Haiku, under a minute). "Give me the cheapest prototype... to show me what you're thinking." Said in 5 sessions. (Ash, Oct 27 to Nov 1; Fuji rulebook 3)
- **Keep what he already built.** Change the part he asked about; a design that throws away his work gets rejected. (learn skills)

## For others

**Briefing someone who decides** (boss, official, partner, teammate)
- They read alone, nobody presenting. They need: what is decided, why, how it looks, what they decide. (brief-page page-shape)
- **Name Ash ("Ash wants to..."), say "you" to the reader,** so it still reads right when forwarded. (brief-page writing-rules "Voice")
- **Always show how the finished thing looks.** "Describing the parts is never enough." (brief-page)
- **Status words stay honest:** a plan is a plan, never proven. Ash's private context stays out. (brief-page)
- **His own team:** plain and direct, no marketing, no hedges. Ask for their ideas; a plan built alone and presented fails. (Ash, Oct 27)

**Persuading an outsider**
- **Quote their asks back,** once, in their words. One answer per ask. (pitch-site writing-rules)
- **Headline: their question, 8 words or fewer.** The answer comes in the first words under it. (pitch-site copy-rules)
- **Collaborative, never credit-taking, never hinting they lack something.** Outcomes are goals until they happen. (pitch-site)
- **Capability, then impact:** every capability ends in what they get. (pitch-site writing-rules)
- **"I" or no pronouns, never the corporate "we".** "There's no 'we', this is 'I'." (Ash, Nov 2; pitch-site copy-rules)
- **Close with one named person,** their email as text you can select, and what they get next. Never "Let's talk". (pitch-site story)

**Teaching students**
- `/write:course-lesson` holds every rule. In short: question-led days, one running student, the impact named, one Do with its time.

**A message to one person** (WhatsApp, email, a DM)
- **Always the three: what you need, why it's worth a yes to them, by when** (keep Ash's own dates exactly). Missing the why? Write your best guess and flag it.
- **Short, warm, plain, in Ash's voice ("I"), WhatsApp length,** no AI tells. Vary how sentences open (not "I'd... I'd... I'd"). Professors and officials by title ("Shraddha Ma'am"). (Ash, Oct 29, drafts to four professors)
- **Candid and numbered** when there's more than one point, like his own message to Dr. SK (Oct 27): what he needs, why, by when.
- Money or anything touchy: name it and say you'll talk about it in person. (Ash, Oct 29)
- A questionnaire: most important question first, one idea each, how to answer, and that partial answers help. (ask-someone)

**Docs for Claude** (skills, CLAUDE.md, tickets)
- `/write:agent-docs` and CONVENTIONS.md "Writing the body" hold the rules: steps first with done tests, say what to do, one source of truth.

## Words and patterns that are out

The scan script catches most of these: `../../../visualize/skills/brief-page/scripts/scan_copy.py` (path from this skill's folder).
- **Dashes:** no em or en dashes. Use a comma, colon, full stop, or "to" in ranges. [3+]
- **Negative parallelism:** "not X but Y", "isn't X, it's Y", "not just". Say the positive. [3+]
- **Lead-ins:** "Here's the thing", "Let's dive in", "It's worth noting". Go straight to the point. [3+]
- **Hype words:** leverage, unlock, journey, empower, crucial, seamless, robust, game-changer, landscape, elevate, holistic, synergy, delve, embark. [3+]
- **Frequency claims:** "most founders", "studies show". [3+]
- **Rhythm tricks:** "Fast. Simple. Powerful.", one-word sentences, matched sentence lengths. [3+]
- **Asides in brackets:** "(made up)", "(this is important)". [3+]

## Read it as Ash

Before showing anything, read it once as Ash would on his phone, tired, between two things. Fix whatever trips any of these:
1. **Can I say what this is for, and what to do next, in the first 5 seconds?**
2. **Is there a line I'd have to read twice, or a word I'd have to look up?**
3. **Is there a list past 3, a paragraph past 3 sentences, or a heading that doesn't say what's under it?**
4. **Did it invent something without saying so?**
5. **Would I have to ask a follow-up question that the page could have answered?**
6. **Is any heading an announcement or a clever label instead of what the section says?**
7. **Is any whole sentence in bold, or did I change a date, name or number Ash gave?**

**Dictation slips** to read through: "topography" means typography, "motive" means motif. (week-sheet briefs)

# The 10 Ways

A topic can be expanded into at least 10 angles. Write 1 pair per way, then rank them. Every pair below appears the way the skill outputs it: a headline and a subhead, already through the craft rules in `headline-craft.md`. Figures and stories in these examples are illustrative.

Ways to apply to every angle:
- Think in headlines first. The headline names the angle and the reader's outcome before any content exists.
- Tie the angle to a desired outcome the reader already has.
- Write for someone a few steps behind the writer. A person who did something once is still ahead of a person who did it zero times.
- A pair can blend 2 ways when that sharpens it.

---

## 1. Tips

**What it does:** Gives the reader an action list for an outcome they want.
**Formula:** The outcome + the reader's starting situation. A count is welcome when it fits.

**Good**
- Headline: 7 Ways to Get Your First Paying Client Without a Portfolio
- Subhead: For freelancers with no past work, ordered by how fast each one gets a reply.
- Why it works: It names the outcome, the situation, and gives the subhead a job (who, by situation) and adds sequencing.

**Bad**
- Headline: Tips for Getting Clients
- Subhead: Follow these tips to grow your business.
- Why it fails: Any client, any business, any reader. The subhead restates the headline and promises a vague result.

---

## 2. Stats

**What it does:** Frames the reader's world as a number, then shows what it means for them. Think of the topic as an industry and ask what would be framed as a stat. One stat leads to more stats.
**Formula:** A surprising number about the reader's situation + the action it points to. Use a real figure from the chat, or invent a placeholder and tag it [invented]. A cited source raises perceived credibility, but never attach an invented figure to a real named source.

**Good**
- Headline: Most Agency Owners Spend 60% of Their Week on Work Nobody Pays Them For
- Subhead: A 7-day time audit shows where the hours go and which 3 tasks to hand off first.
- Why it works: The number makes the reader check their own week, and the subhead promises a method.

**Bad**
- Headline: The Shocking Truth About Time Management
- Subhead: You will not believe what the research says.
- Why it fails: No number, no reader, no claim. "Shocking truth" and "you will not believe" are clickbait with nothing inside.

---

## 3. Steps

**What it does:** Walks the reader from where they are to an outcome they named. The steps must lead somewhere.
**Formula:** How to + a specific destination + a time or starting point.

**Good**
- Headline: How to Build a Client Onboarding System in 1 Weekend
- Subhead: For solo service founders who rebuild the same emails and forms for every new client.
- Why it works: The destination is concrete, the time is bounded, and the subhead names the reader by what they do.

**Bad**
- Headline: Steps to Success in Business
- Subhead: Follow these steps and see results.
- Why it fails: "Success" is no destination. The subhead is a vague guarantee that restates the headline.

---

## 4. Lessons

**What it does:** Shares what you learned from an experience the reader has not had yet. It comes as a pair: lessons from achieving something, and lessons from failing or wasting something. Both are valuable.
**Formula:** What I learned + an outcome + a number. Write both sides. Lead with the stronger one and put the opposite in the Flip line.

**Good**
- Headline: What I Learned Landing 3 Clients in 30 Days With No Portfolio **[invented]**
- Subhead: The 4 messages that got replies and the 6 that never did.
- Flip: What I Learned Wasting $2,000 on Ads Before I Had a Single Client **[invented]**
- Why it works: Each side names an outcome with a number, and the failure side gives the reader something to avoid.

**Bad**
- Headline: Lessons From My Journey
- Subhead: Here is what I learned along the way.
- Why it fails: No outcome and no number. "Journey" is on the avoid list, and the subhead restates the headline.

---

## 5. Benefits

**What it does:** Educates the reader on what happens when they take an action they have not taken. Readers often do not know what they do not know, so the benefit has to be concrete.
**Formula:** What changes + the action + the part of the reader's week or results it changes.

**Good**
- Headline: What Changes When Your Agency Can Run for 1 Week Without You
- Subhead: Fewer decisions through you frees time for sales, sleep, and the conversations you have been avoiding.
- Why it works: The action is specific, the time is bounded, and the benefits are things the reader can picture.

**Bad**
- Headline: The Benefits of Systems
- Subhead: Systems can help you in many ways.
- Why it fails: Which system, for whom, and helping how? The subhead hedges and says nothing.

---

## 6. Mistakes

**What it does:** Speaks to the fear underneath a reader's questions, which is doing something wrong. People want to know what not to do as much as what to do.
**Formula:** The specific stage where the mistake happens + the reader + the cost.

**Good**
- Headline: The 4 Mistakes First-Time Authors Make When They Hire a Cover Designer
- Subhead: Most surface after the files are paid for. Here is what to ask before you sign.
- Why it works: The stage (hiring a designer), the reader (first-time authors), and the cost (paid for files) are all named.

**Bad**
- Headline: Common Mistakes People Make in Business
- Subhead: Avoid these pitfalls and succeed.
- Why it fails: No stage and no reader. The subhead implies a guarantee and fits 10 unrelated pieces.

---

## 7. Reasons

**What it does:** Connects an action to why it matters. It comes as a pair: reasons to do something and reasons not to do something. Reasons work against a belief the reader holds, so the headline names the action.
**Formula:** A number of reasons + the action + what the reader assumes instead. Write both sides. Lead with the stronger one and put the opposite in the Flip line.

**Good**
- Headline: 5 Reasons to Raise Your Prices Before You Add a Single New Client
- Subhead: Each one comes from a bottleneck most solo founders only notice after burning out.
- Flip: 3 Reasons Not to Hire Your First Freelancer Until Your Process Is Written Down
- Why it works: Both sides name an action, and each pushes against something the reader assumes.

**Bad**
- Headline: Reasons You Should Be Successful
- Subhead: Success is possible for everyone who tries.
- Why it fails: No action, no belief it challenges, no reader. The subhead is a platitude.

---

## 8. Questions

**What it does:** Speaks to the voice in the reader's head. Use a question the reader has actually asked, including questions the writer once asked. A question that exposes a contradiction in the reader's situation pulls hardest.
**Formula:** The reader's real question + a subhead that promises the specific answer or gives the proof.

**Good**
- Headline: How Do You Raise Prices When Your Clients Are Also Your Friends?
- Subhead: 3 scripts for the conversation, from founders who raised rates 20% and kept every client.
- Why it works: The question is one the reader would never say aloud, and the subhead delivers a concrete answer with proof.

**Bad**
- Headline: Are You Ready to Change Your Life?
- Subhead: Read on to find out.
- Why it fails: A yes or no question with no reader and no topic. The subhead gives no information.

---

## 9. Examples

**What it does:** Builds pattern recognition and motivation by showing a person or company doing the thing. Positive examples show what good looks like. Negative examples show what bad looks like. Both teach.
**Formula:** A specific case + the outcome with a number + what the reader can take from it.

**Good**
- Headline: How 1 Solo Designer Went From 2 Clients to a 6-Person Studio in 14 Months **[invented]**
- Subhead: The 3 hires, in order, and what each one freed up.
- Why it works: The case has a before, an after, and a time. The subhead promises the sequence the reader can copy.

**Bad**
- Headline: Examples of Successful Founders
- Subhead: Learn from the best.
- Why it fails: No named outcome and no number. "The best" fits everyone, and there is no reader.

---

## 10. Personal story

**What it does:** Tells a story that serves an outcome the writer and reader share. The reader stays because they want the same ending or want to avoid the same mistake. A story told only because the writer lived it loses the reader.
**Formula:** I + a before and after with numbers + the outcome the reader wants. Real or invented, tagged when invented.

**Good**
- Headline: I Worked 70 Hours a Week for 8 Months and Made the Same Money. Then I Fixed 1 Step. **[invented]**
- Subhead: What I found when I timed every task in my agency for 7 days.
- Why it works: The before is felt, the turn is specific, and the reader wants the same discovery.

**Bad**
- Headline: My Story
- Subhead: How I got where I am today.
- Why it fails: No outcome, no number, and nothing the reader wants. It is about the writer, not a shared goal.

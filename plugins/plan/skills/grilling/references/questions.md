# Writing a grilling question

Ash, Nov 5, 2026: the questions must make sense to someone who never saw the chat, and a 7th grader must be able to answer them. The same rule as the study skills (`../../../../learn/skills/study-bba/references/writing-rules.md`, "Questions he answers").

## The test

**Cover everything else on the screen. Can a 7th grader still say what's being asked, and answer it?** If not, rewrite it before sending.

- **Self-contained:** it names the who and the what. No "it", "this", "the fix", "the bigger version" that only the chat explains.
- **One ask:** one question mark, one thing to answer.
- **Open:** starts with what, why, how, which, who or when. Never yes or no.
- **7th-grade words,** under about 25 words.
- **Built from his brain dump:** it reuses his words and his real examples, so he recognises the situation at once.

## Mom Test questions (Layer 1 stories)

Ask about **one real past moment**, never opinions, the future or "would you". The one allowed "if" is slot 4's check: "If <the problem he picked> were fixed, what would still go wrong?"
- **The last time:** "What were you doing right before you ordered food delivery the last time?"
- **The time it worked:** "When was the last week you ate home food most nights, and what was different about that week?"
- **What he's tried:** "What have you already tried to cook at home more, and what made each one stop?"

Pick 1 or 2 that cover **different slots,** so his answer to one doesn't answer the other. Each carries a "Looking for:" line naming the slot.

## Bad and good

- Bad: "Where does the energy go when you learn?" (abstract, he has to guess what's meant)
  Good: "The last time you sat down to read a book to learn, what made you stop?"
- Bad: "What counts as learned for you?" (no situation)
  Good: "What was the last idea from a book that you actually used at work, and how did you first come across it?"
- Bad: "Walk me through the last few." (only the chat explains "the last few")
  Good: "Which of the side-skill articles on your calendar last week did you open, and what made you skip the rest?"
- Bad: "Does this plan look right?" (yes or no)
  Good: "What would you change in this plan before I build the menu card?"
- Bad: "Why the hook fails, and what a month from now looks like?" (two asks)
  Good: one question for each, in separate rounds, only if each fills a slot.

# The look

Ash liked the first book's look: "I love the colours, I love how things are. They can be a little simpler." Keep it.

- **Dark only, a palette per book, from one numbered combination in Wada Sanzo's colour book** (Ash, Nov 3 2026). The colour rules and the script live in `/visualize:match-ash-design`. Run `python ../../../visualize/skills/match-ash-design/scripts/palette.py --place dark --seed "<book title>"` and paste its `:root{...}` block and key-line CSS; the same seed gives the same combination on every rebuild. `--combo N` for one Ash names, `--seed random` for another, `--preset 1|2|3` for an approved preset (Graphite and chalk, Ink and clay, Charcoal and rose), `--key block|band|underline` to force a key-line style. Put the combination's number and names in a comment at the top of the book's CSS.
  - **Never a white or light reading surface** ("I would never want to read things in white"). Grounds are near-black, never tinted blue or green. Every colour in the book can be a pastel or an accent, greens and blues included (Ash, Nov 3 2026); `--p1` is never yellow, because it carries the marker.
  - **The tokens, the same in every book:** `--bg` page, `--bg2` raised surface (the desk, the key-line block, picture boards), `--bg3` higher surface (desk header, bubbles, cards), `--tx` body text, `--mu` muted text, `--ln` hairlines, `--p1` lead pastel (stations, notes card, Send), `--p2` your-side pastel (your label, route chip, Try-it border, move chips), `--p3` concept-name pastel, `--acc` the one strong accent (list markers, the selection dot, the open count), `--on` dark text on a pastel. The script also gives `--acc2` (a second strong colour, for charts), `--onacc` and `--b1` to `--b4` (the book's colours as printed, for big shapes in pictures only). Write every colour in the book through them, the map's script included (read them with `getComputedStyle`).
  - **The script checks contrast** (text 12:1 on `bg` and `bg2`, 10.5:1 on `bg3`, muted text 4.5:1 on `bg2`, dark text 7:1 on every pastel, the accents 3:1 on the ground) and refuses a combination that fails. `--test` checks every combination and the presets.
- **The key line: one sentence per section that matters, and it must read alone.** It names what it is about ("Without a starting line, a split by door and a rule set first, you read each week's number as proof of whatever you already believed", never "Without them, you..."). The script picks one of three styles per book and prints its CSS and HTML: **block** (pulled out on `bg2` with a `p1` bar and a "The point" label), **band** (a soft `p1` band behind the bold sentence, a muted "Why it matters: ..." line under it) or **underline** (`p1` bold text with a dotted underline, the reason kept in the same sentence). At most one per block.
- **Chrome stays quiet** (Ash, Nov 2 2026, on a pink "My notes" pill with an orange count chip: "garbage"). Buttons and counts in the top bar, the rail and the desk sit on `bg2` or `bg3` with `--tx` or `--mu` text and an `--ln` border. A count is plain text, coloured at most with one pastel as text (never a filled chip). Pastel fills are kept for the notes card, the Send button and the concept-name chip in a reply. Never put one coloured shape inside another (a chip inside a pill), and never set `--acc` text on a dark chip inside a pastel.
- **Fonts:** Overpass for headings (700 to 800), Atkinson Hyperlegible for body (17 to 18 px, line height about 1.68), Overpass Mono for labels, kickers and chips. The old serif is out. Hand lettering (Patrick Hand) stays for sketches. Bold lead-ins in `--tx`; list markers in `--acc`; headings in `--tx`.
- **Pictures on dark:** sketches sit on `bg2` boards with pastel fills, `--on` text inside pastel shapes, and `--tx` strokes and labels. No white or paper boards.
- **A little flair, where it carries the message:** the mind map's branches draw in, a sticky part list at the top, "The answer" as a quiet raised block. One moving thing per screen; everything readable at rest and under reduced motion.
- **Pictures only where they explain faster than words.** The six-step journey picture in the first version was judged garbage because its labels were jargon and it read B to A. A picture's labels follow the same rule as the text: what happens, in one to three plain words.
- **The problem map is a moving map, never a list or a grid of boxes** (`moving-map.md`): a motif from Ash's world draws itself on as he scrolls and reveals the unlock order layer by layer, then rests labelled and studyable.
- **Vary a little between chapters** (the picture, the shape of the answer block, the section rhythm) so the book doesn't read as one template. Clarity wins over variety.
- **Check at desktop width.** Load the page in Playwright at 1280 px; screenshot the map at each of its scroll steps (the motion must read), the shelves and each chapter, and look at them. Ash studies on a laptop, so the phone check is one: at 390 px nothing scrolls sideways.
- **Save the HTML in Shasta** under `<Area>/books/`, publish it as a private artifact, and keep the link in the Fuji ticket.

# Ash's taste

Use this to judge any idea the catalogue does not cover, and to pick between two good options. Everything here comes
from what Ash said about real work, mostly in his own words.

## Contents
- What he loves, with the reason
- What he rejects
- How he works
- Judging something new

## What he loves, with the reason

- **Anthropic's craft, with his own identity.** claude.com/product/overview (the timeline rail and the moving mind
  map with connecting lines across data points), the hand-drawn illustrations on claude.com/resources/articles, the
  "Briefing" card's blue-grey with a hand-drawn globe, the global-workspace film's prompt card with a yellow marker.
  He wants that level of care, never Anthropic's own colours.
- **Line drawings that explain.** The Washington Post scrollytelling piece: one line-drawn subject pinned while the
  world changes around it, an x-ray showing what is inside, a pull-back to the bigger picture, labels landing inside
  out, colour arriving one hue at a time.
- **Awwwards sites he picked:** Heron (a blueprint drawing itself on warm paper with one orange accent), Pier Gorelli
  (plot, then build, then live on one canvas), Metic (a build-up that collapses into the call to action), Jithin RP
  (a signature drawn at a speed set by each stroke's length), Townhouse, Blunt Umbrellas, Deferred Damage. Common
  thread: one object holds still while short chapters pass; drafting language; one accent does the pointing.
- **A culturally rooted motif that carries the story.** The kolam from his own photo: a cube of three rhombi with curls,
  three interlaced triple-strand bands, six petal flowers. The Indian way, shown before anyone reads.
- **The snake.** Problem-first cards on one scroll-drawn line, importance-based, "what problem each faculty solves".
- **Clarity that works on a skim:** the reader's asks in their words, then where each is answered (his own PDF model);
  highlighted phrases in colour; circled options; named people drawn in the page's own style.
- **A visual metaphor that holds the information,** so the words don't have to (`../story-plan/references/living-metaphors.md`). The Mauna
  Kea dive: a bathysphere finds a school of fish, a nautilus, a hermit crab and the hotspot, one per value. "A visual
  metaphor can encompass so much detail."
- **Lights that hide things.** Fixed lamps in a scene that switch on and reveal an easter egg (the A S S constellation).
- **A horizontal scroll painting** with a small figure walking the trail underneath as the progress bar.
- **"Creative as fuck. Yet, still being clear."** Clarity wins every tie.

## What he rejects

- The JAIN navy hero (#161A2E) and any navy night hero; green anywhere; Anthropic's clay and ivory as a palette.
- A first screen where the next section bleeds in; a headline packed with data instead of saying what the section is about.
- A line running behind the copy for the whole page ("distracting from the message").
- Horizontal step strips ("far too lazy"); team lists ("garbage"); a generic avatar; a vague last step ("absolute garbage").
- Choose-your-own-adventure, role pickers, "pick your layer", a paper leave-behind. He wants it "fed down the throat".
- Taking credit or implying the client lacks something ("we're not roasting them"); dated plans nobody committed to.
- Buzzwords in isolation: every capability must say why the reader should care.
- Videos in the page; motion built in code is fine.
- Anything that looks like "a random HTML file" or a template.
- Labelled concentric rings or stacked bands for an idea that matters ("such a boring way to show things").
- A caption box that pops up when a marker is clicked ("so boring"); a scene full of markers to hunt ("clicky").
- A glow that follows the cursor ("garbage"); hover that glides or fades ("not a fan of how smooth it is").

## How he works

- He dictates by voice: names arrive garbled (kolam as "cobra", "column", "Cura"; TVS as "TBS"; Jinto as "Gento").
- Plan first, then a small prototype he can react to, then the full build. He reacts to what he sees, in long paragraphs.
- He distrusts unaided taste: study real references (raw site code, video frames) before designing.
- He wants the alternatives recorded, not only the chosen route.
- He wants a quality check by reviewer agents before he looks, and he runs `/verify` himself.
- When he is away and says "don't ask", decide, log the decision, and keep going.

## Judging something new

Ask, in order:
1. Does it make the reader understand faster? If not, cut it, however good it looks.
2. Could it belong to any client? If yes, it is generic: rebuild it from the client's own world.
3. Would it sit comfortably next to the Washington Post piece and claude.com? Calm, drawn, explanatory motion.
4. Is there one accent doing one job, and is everything readable at rest?
5. Would Ash call it lazy (a strip, a list, a card row) or distracting (decoration that moves)?
When nothing here or in the catalogue fits, search award galleries (Awwwards, Godly, Codrops) and explainer
journalism (The Pudding, NYT, Reuters), and keep only what passes these five questions.

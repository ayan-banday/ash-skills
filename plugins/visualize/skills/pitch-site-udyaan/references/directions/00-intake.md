# Directions: intake, colour rules, deriving a palette

The direction sets the motif, palette, type, texture and motion of the whole site. Ask once; if Ash has no inspiration, offer 3 to 5 from the library below. Each direction has its own file in this folder; open only the chosen one.

## Part 1. The intake script

### 1.1 The one question

The skill asks exactly one short question, once, before any design work:

> **"Any inspiration for the look? A craft, a material or a place, like the kolam we used for TVS. Or say 'show me'."**

Then it branches on the answer:

- **Ash names something in this library** ("kolam", "make it like a metro map"): confirm in one line and build. Example: "Going with Transit: three lines, one hub. Hub is the client, lines are the three needs."
- **Ash names something new** ("Chettinad tiles", "Kanjeevaram silk", "cricket scorecard"): run Part 3 (derive a palette from any subject), then build a story device by answering four questions about the motif: what is its centre, what are its strands, what are its repeated parts, what are its levels or passes. Show the result as one option card for a yes or no.
- **Ash says "show me", "no idea", "you pick", or gives no answer:** present 3 to 5 options (1.2).
- **Ash says "mix two":** allowed only when both directions share a temperature (both warm-craft or both cool-modern). Motif from one, palette from the other. Never mix two cultures' motifs.

### 1.2 How to present the options

Each option is three lines: the name, one sentence a non-designer can picture, and a swatch line of six chips (ground, paper, tint-1, tint-2, tint-3, accent-1). In the HTML the swatch line renders as six 18px squares; in chat it is the hex list.

Example, for a JAIN University proposal to an EV battery maker in Hosur (Tamil Nadu):

> **1. Drafting.** A pencil drawing of a wheel hub on pale vellum, with dimension arrows and a title block.
> `#2B2927 #F0EDE5 #C6D3E2 #EDE0B8 #EBC6C0 #B3322E`
>
> **2. Circuit.** Copper traces fan out from one black chip across amber film to gold pads.
> `#2A211C #F2EEE6 #F0D2A0 #CDD3DE #E9C1AD #A04A28`
>
> **3. Kolam.** White rice-powder loops drawn around a dot grid on a dark wooden threshold.
> `#3A2B22 #F1EDE4 #ECD9A6 #C6CFDA #DDB09A #9E3B2A`
>
> **4. Transit.** Three coloured lines that only run straight or at 45 degrees, meeting at one ringed hub.
> `#2A2522 #F3F0E9 #F2DE9C #C2D5E8 #E3C2D3 #BC3429`
>
> Which one? Or name your own.

The first option is the recommendation. The skill says nothing else: no pros and cons, no paragraph per option. If Ash asks "why", answer in one line from the direction's "Suits" entry.

### 1.3 How to choose which 3 to 5 to offer

Run these steps in order. They take seconds and they keep the list honest.

1. **Read the brief for four facts:** the client's industry, the tone the proposal needs, who will read it, and the proposal's shape (how many needs, phases, workstreams, or parties).
2. **Apply the hard filters.**
   - A culture-specific direction (kolam, ajrakh, Warli, Madhubani, patola, truck art, kumiko, sashiko, girih, azulejo) is offered only if it passes the **connection test**: the client, the partner, the place, or the subject has a real tie to that culture or craft. Hosur passes for kolam; a Pune fintech does not.
   - Drop any direction whose "Avoid when" matches the brief.
   - Drop the direction already used for this same client, unless Ash asks for it.
3. **Score the rest, 0 to 2 on each of four things:** industry fit, tone fit, readability for this audience, and story fit (does the motif's structure match the proposal's shape?). Story fit decides ties. Shape matches:
   - **Three needs:** kolam (three loops), Bauhaus (three forms), riso (three drums), truck art (three plates), madhubani (three bands).
   - **Phases over time:** Warli (spiral turns), Swiss (rings), patola (rows), herbarium (branches), truck art (chain links).
   - **Parallel workstreams:** transit (lines), circuit (trace groups), kumiko (six sectors), origami (fold bands).
   - **Process passes or stages:** ajrakh (print passes), riso (drum passes), drafting (views).
   - **Two parties becoming one:** patola (warp and weft), Madhubani (two fish), azulejo (tiles meeting), riso (overprint).
   - **Principles or values:** girih (ten kites), kumiko (infill pieces).
4. **Build a spread from the top scorers:** one **rooted** option (culture or craft tied to the client), one **industry-native** option (a material from the client's own world: drafting for auto, circuit for chips, herbarium for agriculture), one **clean modern** option (Swiss, Bauhaus or transit), and one or two **wild cards** (riso, Memphis, origami, truck art) only if the tone allows play. Never offer three from the same family.
5. **Order by total score.** Highest first; it becomes the recommendation.

- **Manufacturing, engineering, infrastructure:** drafting, kumiko, Bauhaus, circuit, Swiss.

## Part 2. Colour rules

### 2.1 Roles

Every direction fills the same 13 roles, so the site template never changes; only the tokens do.

- **ground, ground-2:** the dark surfaces (hero, one mid-page section, closing). Warm, never navy.
- **paper, card:** the reading surfaces. Off-white with the material's warmth; card is the lighter of the two.
- **ink:** all body text. A near-black tinted by the direction's darkest material.
- **muted:** secondary text on paper and card only.
- **line:** hairlines and borders, always ink at 14% alpha.
- **tint-1, tint-2, tint-3:** chalky section blocks, one flat colour per section.
- **marker:** the highlighter behind key phrases, and the one bright colour allowed on dark sections.
- **accent-1, accent-2:** the strong colours, used rarely.

**What "chalky" means in numbers.** Measured on the kolam tints, the TVS team chips, and Anthropic's own tints: OKLCH lightness 0.84 to 0.90 and chroma 0.02 to 0.07 (pale yellows can reach 0.09). In HSB terms that is saturation 10 to 35% at brightness 85 to 95%. Do not judge chalkiness with HSL saturation: pale yellows read 60 to 80% in HSL while looking soft on screen.

### 2.2 The 60-30-10 split for a proposal site

- **60% paper family.** Paper and card carry all long reading: the body, cards, tables of numbers, team.
- **30% colour blocks.** The tints and the dark ground, as full-width flat section backgrounds. At most two dark sections per page (hero and closing, or hero and one mid-page "big number" section). Never two adjacent sections with the same tint.
- **10% strong.** Motif lines, marker highlights and accents. Within that 10%: accent-1 appears at most once per screen (a single number, one badge's lit part, one button), and the marker highlights one to three phrases per section, never a whole paragraph.

A section sequence that works for most proposals: ground (hero) → paper → tint-1 → paper → tint-2 → card → ground (big number) → paper → tint-3 → ground (closing).

### 2.3 Category and team chips

Chips (team members, categories, workstreams) must look like a family: **equal lightness, equal chroma, evenly spaced hues, green removed.**

- Use the three tints when there are three or fewer categories.
- For more, generate in OKLCH at L 0.87, C 0.05, with hues spread evenly over the arc that skips 100 to 210 (yellow-green, green, teal, aqua). Ink text on every chip passes 10:1.
- Assign colours in a stable order (sorted by name or by org chart), so colours never shuffle between renders.
- The TVS chips measured OKLCH L 0.85 to 0.89, which is why they read as one set.

```js
// Equal-lightness chips with the green band removed. Vanilla JS, no deps.
function oklchToHex(L, C, h) {
  const a = C * Math.cos(h * Math.PI / 180), b = C * Math.sin(h * Math.PI / 180);
  const l = (L + .3963377774 * a + .2158037573 * b) ** 3, m = (L - .1055613458 * a - .0638541728 * b) ** 3, s = (L - .0894841775 * a - 1.291485548 * b) ** 3;
  return '#' + [4.0767416621 * l - 3.3077115913 * m + .2309699292 * s, -1.2684380046 * l + 2.6097574011 * m - .3413193965 * s, -.0041960863 * l - .7034186147 * m + 1.707614701 * s]
    .map(x => { x = Math.min(1, Math.max(0, x)); x = x <= .0031308 ? 12.92 * x : 1.055 * x ** (1 / 2.4) - .055; return Math.round(x * 255).toString(16).padStart(2, '0'); }).join('');
}
function chips(n, { L = .87, C = .05, skip = [100, 210], offset = 10 } = {}) {
  const span = 360 - (skip[1] - skip[0]);
  return [...Array(n)].map((_, i) => oklchToHex(L, C, (skip[1] + offset + i * span / n) % 360));
}
// chips(9) ->
// #b1ddeb #bbd8f4 #cad2f6 #dbcdf0 #e8c9e4 #f1c7d3 #f4c8c3 #efccb6 #e5d2b0
// ink #241D18 on these: 10.96 to 11.40:1; OKLCH hues 219, 247, 275, 304, 330, 359, 26, 54, 82
```

### 2.4 Dark sections (the ink and paper flip)

Measured across all 20 palettes:

- **Text on ground is paper.** Paper on ground measures 10.95 to 14.21:1.
- **Secondary text on ground is paper at 72% opacity** (mix paper into ground). Measures 6.46 to 7.99:1. Muted is never used on ground.
- **Hairlines on ground are paper at 14%.**
- **The motif on ground is drawn in paper** (or the direction's powder or thread colour).
- **The marker is the only bright colour on ground,** as an underline, a number, or a lit badge part. It measures 7.75 to 11.04:1.
- **Accents never carry text on ground.** They measure 1.53 to 4.13:1, which fails. Use them only as large shapes, if at all.
- **No tint blocks inside a dark section.** Chips on a dark section keep their tint fill and ink text.

Implement it as a token flip, so components never need dark variants:

```css
.on-ground{background:var(--ground);color:var(--paper);
  --text:var(--paper);--text-2:color-mix(in srgb,var(--paper) 72%,var(--ground));
  --rule:color-mix(in srgb,var(--paper) 14%,transparent);
  --m1:var(--paper);--m2:var(--ground-2);--m3:var(--card);--bgc:var(--ground)}
```

### 2.5 Never

- Pure #FFFFFF or #000000, or grey with no warmth.
- Green in any role, including mint, teal and aqua chips.
- A navy or blue-black hero (Ash rejected #161A2E). Blue lives in tints and accents only.
- Anthropic's values: ivory #FAF9F5, #F0EEE6, #141413, cloud #C5D3E0, peach #EBC9B7, manilla #EBDBBC, clay #D97757. A script checks every palette here against them.
- Gradients between sections, or a gradient as a section background. Each section is one flat colour.
- Muted text on a tint: it drops to 3.1:1 in this library (kolam muted on laterite). On tint blocks all text is ink.
- Accent as body text when it measures under 4.5:1 on paper (Riso pink, Memphis red). Those two are flagged as display-only.
- A tint on a tint, or a card on a tint without a 1px line.
- The marker as a large fill (more than about 15% of a screen).
- Multiply-blending blue over yellow: it makes green. Overprint effects use pink, purple and yellow, or stack shapes opaquely.
- Colour as the only signal in a badge. The lit part also changes opacity, and the section label names it.
- Random hues for chips.
- A culture's sacred imagery or script as decoration: deities, temple or mosque silhouettes, Arabic or Sanskrit text nobody on the team can read.

## Part 3. Derive a palette from any subject

Use this when Ash names an inspiration that is not in the library, or to re-tune a library palette for a client.

### 3.1 The method

1. **Name the world.** One place and moment where the story happens. Not "India"; "the threshold of a South Indian home at dawn."
2. **List 8 to 12 materials in that world,** physical things with real colours: wood, powder, stone, dye, metal, paper, flowers, cloth.
3. **Sample their raw colours** from a photo or a museum image (the reference link of a direction is a good source).
4. **Assign the structural roles by behaviour.**
   - The darkest structural material becomes **ground** (wood, iron, lacquer, mud wall). Ground-2 is the same hue 5 to 8 points lighter.
   - The drawing or writing medium becomes **paper** on the dark ground (powder, rice paste, thread, chalk).
   - The reading surface becomes **paper and card**: an off-white leaning toward the material's hue, never pure.
   - **Ink** is the darkest material pushed toward neutral: OKLCH L about 0.22, chroma under 0.02, hue kept.
   - **Muted** is ink mixed about 55% toward paper, then checked to be at least 4.5:1 on paper.
5. **Push three section colours to chalk.** Keep each material's hue, set OKLCH L to 0.86 to 0.89, cut chroma to about 40% of the raw value and cap it at 0.065. Then nudge by eye toward the material's daylight colour.
6. **Pick one marker:** the brightest material in the world (marigold, turmeric, brass, signal yellow). It is a highlighter, so ink on marker must be at least 7:1.
7. **Pick one or two accents:** the ritual or strong colour (kumkum, lac, cobalt), plus one cool or warm counterweight. If an accent will ever carry text, it must be at least 4.5:1 on paper.
8. **Check contrast** with the script in 3.3. Body pairs must pass 4.5:1: ink on paper, ink on card, ink on each tint, muted on paper, paper on ground.
9. **Name tokens after the materials** (`--wood`, `--haldi`, `--kumkum`) and alias them to the roles. The names keep the story in the code and make the palette explainable to a client.

### 3.2 Worked example: JAIN University to TVS (kolam, "threshold")

1. **World:** the threshold of a South Indian home at dawn, where the kolam is drawn.
2. **Materials:** teak door frame, rice powder, swept earth, turmeric smeared on the step, kumkum dot, indigo-washed wall, laterite stone, marigold string, cotton paper.
3. **Raw samples, then the chalk step** (computed: L to 0.87, chroma to 40% capped at 0.065, hue kept), then the final hand-tuned pick:

- **haldi, turmeric on the step:** raw `#D99A1E` (OKLCH 0.73 / 0.145 / 78) → chalk step `#EAD0AA` → final tint-1 haldi `#ECD9A6` (OKLCH 0.89 / 0.069; ink on it 11.90:1)
- **neel, indigo-washed wall:** raw `#4B6A9B` (OKLCH 0.52 / 0.086 / 259) → chalk step `#C7D5EB` → final tint-2 neel `#C6CFDA` (OKLCH 0.85 / 0.018; ink on it 10.55:1)
- **laterite stone:** raw `#A8553A` (OKLCH 0.55 / 0.116 / 39) → chalk step `#F1CBBE` → final tint-3 laterite `#DDB09A` (OKLCH 0.79 / 0.061; ink on it 8.51:1)
- **kumkum dot:** raw `#9E2B25` (OKLCH 0.47 / 0.152 / 28) → chalk step `#FAC6BE` → final kept strong as accent-1 kumkum `#9E3B2A` (OKLCH 0.49 / 0.136; ink on it 2.46:1)
- **marigold string:** raw `#F2A516` (OKLCH 0.78 / 0.160 / 75) → chalk step `#EDCFA6` → final kept bright as marker `#F2C94C` (OKLCH 0.85 / 0.146; ink on it 10.47:1)
- **teak door frame:** raw `#5A3B26` (OKLCH 0.38 / 0.055 / 54) → chalk step `#E0D1C7` → final darkened to ground wood `#3A2B22` (OKLCH 0.30 / 0.027; ink on it 1.23:1)

4. **Structural roles:** ground = wood #3A2B22, ground-2 = wood-2 #47362B, the drawing medium = powder #F4F1EA, reading surfaces = paper #F1EDE4 and card #FAF7F0, ink = #241D18 (teak pushed to near-black), muted = #6B6158.
5. **Marker:** marigold, #F2C94C (ink on it 10.47:1). **Accents:** kumkum #9E3B2A (5.77:1 on paper) and indigo #3F4A7A (7.27:1 on paper).
6. **Contrast check:** see the kolam entry below; every body pair passes, the lowest is muted on paper at 5.17:1.
7. **Tokens:** `--wood --wood-2 --powder --paper --card --ink --muted --haldi --neel --laterite --marker --kumkum --indigo`, aliased to the 13 roles.

Note on tint-3: laterite #DDB09A sits at OKLCH L 0.79, a little darker than the chalk band. It was kept because the proven site used it and ink still measures 8.51:1 on it. New palettes should stay inside 0.84 to 0.90.

### 3.3 The contrast checker (Python, standard library)

```python
def lum(h):
    c = [int(h.lstrip('#')[i:i+2], 16) / 255 for i in (0, 2, 4)]
    c = [x / 12.92 if x <= .04045 else ((x + .055) / 1.055) ** 2.4 for x in c]
    return .2126 * c[0] + .7152 * c[1] + .0722 * c[2]
def ratio(a, b):
    hi, lo = sorted((lum(a), lum(b)), reverse=True)
    return (hi + .05) / (lo + .05)
p = dict(ground='#3A2B22', paper='#F1EDE4', card='#FAF7F0', ink='#241D18', muted='#6B6158',
         tint1='#ECD9A6', tint2='#C6CFDA', tint3='#DDB09A')
pairs = [('ink','paper'), ('ink','card'), ('ink','tint1'), ('ink','tint2'), ('ink','tint3'),
         ('muted','paper'), ('paper','ground')]
for a, b in pairs:
    r = ratio(p[a], p[b]); print(f"{a}/{b} {r:.2f} {'PASS' if r >= 4.5 else 'FAIL'}")
```

# Examples: good and bad, side by side

Each pair is the same content told two ways. The bad half is real: Ash saw it and said what was wrong, or it is the
film-maker's own counter-example. Copy the reasons, not the pictures.

## Ash's builds

| The content | Bad | Why it failed | Good | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ash's four values | Four labelled concentric rings under a mountain | "Such a boring way to show things." The labels did all the work; the rings said only "these nest" | The dive under Mauna Kea: a fish school, a nautilus, a hermit crab, the hotspot, found one by one | Each creature's real behaviour is the value; the searchlight going deeper is the thesis; one line each is enough |
| The summit's memories | Eight numbered stops scattered over the scene; click one and words write into the sky | "Still clicky, still too much information, still not like a story." A caption system, not a metaphor | One forward control, one stop per step, told in order, earlier parts dimmed but visible | The reader follows instead of hunting; the picture builds |
| What each thing means | A caption box that pops up when a marker is clicked | "So boring." It hides the meaning behind a click and reads as a form | The line writes itself beside the part, after the part moves | Act, then words: the picture explains first, the words confirm |
| Hidden extras | A glow that follows the cursor and reveals a hidden layer | "Kind of garbage." It fights the reader's hand and hides things at random | Fixed lamps in the scene that switch on at night and reveal an easter egg | Loved: deliberate, findable, never needed |
| Hover | Things that glide and fade when the cursor passes | "I'm not a fan of how smooth it is." Feels like a template | Hover snaps: the change happens at once | Only moves between places ease |
| The mountain range | Mauna Kea drawn beautifully, the mountain itself as the metaphor | Pretty, but "the point is to get the philosophy behind it"; the mountain said little | The ocean under the mountain, where each depth means something | The meaning lives in a world with parts that behave |
| A team or capability list (TVS) | A list of departments, or a horizontal step strip | "Garbage", "far too lazy" | The snake: problem-first cards on one drawn line | One line carries order and importance |

## From the films

| The content | Bad | Why it fails | Good | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A heist plan | Characters explain the plan in dialogue, then do it | A "radio play": you can follow it with your eyes closed | *Better Call Saul* skips the planning and shows the job; we piece the plan together | Active viewing; the visuals carry the subtext |
| A couple can't have a child | A scene where they talk about it | Long, and tells instead of shows | *Up*: the nursery being painted, then the doctor and her lowered head, two shots | A hard thing said in two frames, no paragraph between |
| Years passing | A caption: "Forty years later" | Tells, and breaks the spell | *Up*: Carl's tie, re-knotted again and again, the hands older each time | Repetition with change shows time |
| Whether a character died | Another murder on screen | Repeats what we know | *No Country for Old Men*: the killer checks his boots for blood | A planted detail pays off; the viewer completes it |
| The end of a life story | A final speech | Says what the picture already showed | *Up*: Carl alone in the church where it began | Full circle tells the viewer it is over |
| A video about growing | Opening on "YouTube is hard" | Starts at the general point; the video seems to be about YouTube | Opening on a friend asking "what would make you proud of a week?", with dying houseplants through it | Starts at the strongest point; the plants carry the second layer |

## Patterns to spot in a draft

- **Words that narrate the picture:** "Here we see the truck arrive." Cut; write what it means.
- **Two lit things on one screen,** or a lit thing and a moving thing that are different parts.
- **A screen with no Bright:** a paragraph alone, or a picture with nothing leading the eye.
- **The same tension three screens running:** three equal cards, three equal stops.
- **A close that is new:** the last frame shows a scene the reader has never seen, so nothing resolves.
- **A metaphor that needs a key:** if a legend explains the creatures, recast them.
- **Hidden essentials:** anything the reader needs behind a hover, a click or a lamp.

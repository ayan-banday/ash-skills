# Living metaphors: let the picture hold the information

How to show an idea as a world the reader travels through, where each part of the idea is a creature or object
whose real behaviour proves it. The metaphor carries the definition, the example and the memory hook at once, so the
page needs a fraction of the words. Plan the journey as a storyboard (`storyboard.md`). In pitch-site it is
catalogue entry ARC-33 ★ Expedition through a living metaphor.

## The idea

Ash, after the first build: "the point is to get the philosophy behind it: that we can use a visual metaphor to
express information instead of having to write information." And: "a visual metaphor can encompass so much detail."

Proven on The Range (2026-10-19), a personal map of Ash's growth. Four values were first drawn as four labelled,
concentric rings under a mountain: "such a boring way to show things." The same four values were then drawn as a dive
under Mauna Kea, where a bathysphere's searchlight found one creature per layer:

- **Goals**, the sunlight zone: a school of fish, which turns as one when the light finds it.
- **Identity**, the twilight zone: a nautilus, which lives in the newest chamber it built.
- **Acquired values**, the midnight zone: a hermit crab, which wears a shell it found.
- **Natural values**, under the floor: the Hawaiian hotspot, the heat that built the whole island and never moves.

Ash loved it. Nothing about the content changed; only the picture started doing the explaining.

This works for clients too: a client's process, values, risks or model cast from their own world (their sea, their
farm, their factory floor). It is strongest for anything abstract with 3 to 6 parts: values, a mental model, a
framework, layers of a system, stages of a change.

## The test: does the picture carry the information?

Run all five before building. If one fails, recast or use a plainer pattern.

1. **A real, checkable fact maps onto the idea.** The nautilus really adds chambers and lives in the newest one, so it
   can be identity. If the link needs a paragraph, it is decoration. Cut it.
2. **Cover the words.** Someone looking at the picture alone can guess what each part means, or at least that the
   parts differ in kind and order.
3. **Parts differ by what they do,** never only by a label or a tint. A school, a shell-builder, a borrower and a heat
   source are four different behaviours.
4. **One world for the whole set.** All ocean, all forest, all sky. Mixing worlds turns a metaphor into clip art.
5. **The way you look is part of the claim.** On the dive the searchlight is attention, so seeing deeper takes
   more light: "The more aware you are, the deeper you can see." The interaction is the thesis.

## The method

1. **Name the idea's shape.** Depth (how far down you can see), growth over time, borrowed against innate, moving
   together, shedding, exchange, balance.
2. **Pick a world where that shape physically exists.** Depth: ocean zones, rock strata, a dig. Growth: a reef, a
   forest, a city. Altitude: sky layers, a climb. Exchange: a market, a reef's cleaning station.
3. **Cast one thing per part by its behaviour,** from the bank below or from the client's own world. Check every fact
   in a written source before it goes on screen.
4. **Choose the vehicle and the verb.** The reader does one thing to travel (dive, climb, dig, melt, weigh). No two
   expeditions on one page share a verb.
5. **Make the instrument carry the thesis.** The light, the rope, the scale or the lens is the main idea, so the
   journey proves it without a sentence.
6. **Cut the words.** Per stop: the part's name, one line that states the creature's real fact, and at most one
   quote. Twenty words or fewer. The picture holds the rest.

## Tell it as a story, not a set of buttons

Ash on the first build: "still clicky, still too much information, still not like a story we're telling." Plan the
journey as a storyboard first (`storyboard.md`, which re-tells this dive as seven shots). Rules:

- **Open on the thesis, close on it.** One sentence before the journey says what it will prove; the finished picture
  at the end proves it, and the sentence returns.
- **The order is told, not chosen.** Surface to floor, root to crown, first to last. Drive it with the reader's
  scroll (a pinned stage, one stop per scroll step) or one forward control. Markers to click are shortcuts, never the
  main way through. No hotspots scattered around a scene for the reader to hunt.
- **One thing changes per step.** The previous part stays visible but dimmer, so the picture accumulates.
- **Act, then words.** The part does its small move first (the school turns, the crab steps, the heat flares), then
  its line writes itself, whole words about 40ms apart.
- **End on the whole metaphor lit at once,** readable as one picture with every part named, in the opening's own
  framing (full circle). That frame is the screenshot someone remembers.
- **Hidden extras are allowed, never needed.** Fixed lamps in the scene that switch on and reveal an easter egg
  were liked; a glow that follows the cursor was not. Nothing the reader needs lives in the hidden layer.

## Casting bank

Starting points, by shape. Verify each fact before use and state it on screen in one line. Skip anything popular
but disputed (for example, the "bamboo grows roots for five years" story).

| Shape of the idea | Cast | The real fact that carries it |
|---|---|---|
| How deep you can see | Ocean zones: sunlight, twilight, midnight, floor | Less light reaches each zone; life differs by depth |
| Built over time (identity, habits) | Nautilus | Adds chambers as it grows and lives in the newest one |
| Built over time, by many | Coral reef | Built by generations of tiny animals on their predecessors |
| Built slowly, one drop at a time | Stalactite | Grows from minerals left by each drip |
| Borrowed from outside (acquired values) | Hermit crab | Wears a shell it found, and moves to a bigger one |
| Borrowed from the surroundings | Decorator crab | Sticks bits of its habitat onto its shell |
| Innate, the source of everything | The Hawaiian hotspot | Heat from deep below built the islands; it stays put while the plate moves |
| Innate, the strongest part | Heartwood | The dead, hardened centre of a trunk that holds the tree up |
| Moving together (goals, alignment) | School of fish | Turns as one, with no single leader |
| Moving together, sharing the hard part | Geese in a V | The lead bird drops back and another takes over |
| Letting go of an old self | Snake shedding | Sheds its whole skin as it grows |
| Growth means a soft, risky moment | Crab moulting | Is soft and exposed until the new shell hardens |
| Fair exchange | Cleaner fish and grouper | Removes parasites and is fed; the big fish waits its turn |
| One part holds the system | Sea otter as keystone | Eats urchins, which would otherwise strip the kelp forest |
| Hearing the signal under the noise | Owl | Hunts by sound in the dark |
| Change that needs a crisis | Lodgepole pine | Some cones open only in the heat of a fire |

## Vehicles and verbs

Dive (a bathysphere on a cable), climb (a rope team), dig (a trowel and a section wall), descend (a lift shaft),
tilt (a mirror or water surface), melt (a glacier into rivers), weigh (a scale), release (lanterns rising), plan (a
route card, camp by camp), launch (a rocket), zoom (a microscope or telescope), sail (a chart and a course), grow
(time-lapse rings).

## Build mechanics

Proven in `assets/examples/range-src/frag-11-maunakea.html` (the part below the waterline; the summit above it is
the counter-example, see What failed) with `frag-01-engine.html`.

- **A tall scene, a moving camera.** Draw the whole world in one SVG taller than the screen (the dive: 1600 × 1800)
  and show it through a 1600 × 900 `viewBox`. The camera is the `viewBox`, moved with one eased tween (650 to
  1500ms, ease-in-out) between stops. It is not page scroll and not parallax.
- **The vehicle and the gauge travel with the camera.** Translate them by the camera's offset so they hold still on
  screen; a cable or trail stretches behind the vehicle.
- **The gauge lists every stop at rest,** so the whole set is readable before the journey starts. Each tick jumps
  there; a marker shows where you are.
- **Parts wait dim until found.** Every part sits at about 25% opacity; arriving switches it on with a stepped
  flicker (`steps(1)` keyframes), never a fade. The instrument's light is a gradient cone with `mix-blend-mode:
  screen`, angled per stop.
- **Words are written into the scene,** beside the part, with a halo stroke (`paint-order: stroke`) in the
  background colour. No caption boxes.
- **The title steps aside while inside.** Hide the panel head's title (keep its space) during the journey; the
  progress control stays.
- **Finished state.** Under `?final=1` and reduced motion, the `viewBox` shows the full height, every part is lit and
  every line written.
- **Hover and controls answer at once;** only the camera move eases (rule 16 in
  `../pitch-site/references/motion.md`).

## What failed

Each verdict is Ash's, from The Range prototype.

- **Four labelled concentric rings for four values:** "such a boring way to show things."
- **Click a marker, a caption box pops up:** "so boring."
- **Numbered stops whose words write into the sky:** better, but "relatively not that creative": a caption system,
  not a metaphor. Still clicky, still too many words.
- **A soft glow that follows the cursor and reveals a hidden layer:** "kind of garbage". Replaced by fixed lamps.
- **Hover that glides and fades:** "I'm not a fan of how smooth it is."
- **The mountain itself as the metaphor:** pretty, but it said little. The meaning lived in what was under it.

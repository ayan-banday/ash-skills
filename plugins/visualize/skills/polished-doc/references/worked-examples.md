# Worked examples

Three documents taken through the skill, each a different size. Use them to calibrate how much to change.

## Contents
- A short memo: words only, no visuals
- A two-page proposal: two visuals, one table, one decision
- A speech draft: voice-first edits, reviewer feedback mapped

## A short memo: words only, no visuals

**Before**

> **Subject: Update on the onboarding situation**
>
> **Overview**
> Hi all, just wanted to give a quick update on where things are with onboarding. It's not really about the tools, it's more about the process, I think. New hires are kind of taking a while to get set up, probably around 9 days on average, which is something that we should maybe look into at some point.
>
> **Next steps**
> We could potentially look at having a checklist, or perhaps a buddy system, or maybe both. Let me know your thoughts.

**After**

> **Subject: New hires take 9 days to set up**
>
> **The delay comes from our process**
> New hires take about 9 days to get set up. The cause is the process, so new tools would leave it unchanged.
>
> **Pick a checklist, a buddy, or both**
> Two fixes are on the table: a setup checklist, and a buddy for each new hire. ==Reply by Friday== with the one you prefer, or both.

**What changed, and why**
1. Kept the shape: same subject line, two sections, same facts.
2. Both headings now give the answer; "Overview" and "Next steps" named slots.
3. Cut seven hedges: kind of, probably, maybe, maybe, at some point, potentially, perhaps.
4. "It's not really about the tools, it's more about the process" became a plain cause.
5. Cut the setup line ("just wanted to give a quick update").
6. **Flagged one addition:** "Reply by Friday" is new, highlighted, and listed for the writer to confirm.
7. Added no visual and no cover. Two short sections need neither.

## A two-page proposal: two visuals, one table, one decision

The writer's draft said: replies to new leads are slow, here is a rota to fix it, please approve. The skill kept that order and produced:

- **Title gives the answer:** "Reply to every new lead within one hour".
- **Lede with the problem and the fix in two sentences**, bold on "19 hours" and "one person on lead duty each weekday".
- **Section 1, "A reply within the hour makes a booked call six times as likely".** The writer had real counts (5 of 11 fast replies booked, 4 of 53 slow ones), so a dot split shows them. One customer story (Priya) stays as two sentences of text; drawing her would add nothing.
- **Section 2, "Leads wait for the morning sort before anyone owns them".** A Today / Proposed flow, with the waiting step in the alert colour and "Up to 24 hours" written on it.
- **Section 3, "One person on lead duty each weekday removes the wait".** Three numbered steps, the rota as a small table (a schedule is a look-up), the cost in one sentence, then a callback: "Priya would have heard from Sara by 11:14 on Tuesday."
- **Section 4, "Four Fridays of numbers will show whether it works".** Text only; a measurement plan has no shape worth drawing.
- **Decision box:** "Approve the rota by Friday 23 October".

Two visuals in two pages, each one doing work the text could not do as fast.

## A speech draft: voice-first edits, reviewer feedback mapped

A TEDx draft with four reviewer notes: too much technical detail in one part, a jump from engineering to business, a vague move into an Arnold Schwarzenegger example, and no impact for the speaker's framework.

| Reviewer note | Edit | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Too much detail (DC motors, torque, quantum chromodynamics) | Cut the loop-the-loop derivation and "DC"; kept "torque" because the ending calls back to it; replaced the vague complaint with one named thing, quantum field theory | 4, 5 |
| Engineering to business jump | Built the bridge from the speaker's own words: "A company is a machine too. Its parts are people" | 8 |
| Vague move to Arnold | Ended the section on the question Arnold answers; rewrote the exit so Arnold's choices are the framework before it is named | 8 |
| No impact | "That's how I built my first business." No statistic needed | 7 |

Then a voice pass across the whole speech: hedges, five negative parallelisms (two of them introduced by the editor's own earlier fixes), setup lines, and a "most people" claim. Kept on purpose: "Sometimes" where cutting it made the line false, "I might get the answers wrong" because the next line depends on it, and the speaker's strange lines ("bones wanna leave my body", "full cartoon hypnosis").

The lesson: check your own edits with the same rules. Two of the five negative parallelisms came from the first round of fixes.
